Consumers

A consumer reads records and remembers how far it has read. A group of consumers shares the partitions of a topic. Most production problems with Kafka are consumer problems: lag, rebalances, and records processed twice or not at all.

offset 0

What it is and why it exists

A consumer is the client library that reads records from Kafka. It asks a broker for records from a partition, starting at an offset. The broker does not push records and does not track what each consumer has seen. The consumer pulls, and the consumer keeps its own position.

One consumer is often too slow for a busy topic. So consumers join a consumer group, identified by group.id. Kafka gives each partition to exactly one consumer in the group. Two consumers in the same group never read the same partition at the same time. Two different groups each read everything, without affecting each other.

The group saves its position as a committed offset for each partition. After a crash or a restart, the next owner of the partition continues from the committed offset. When and how you commit decides if a failure loses records or repeats them.

The one-sentence version. A consumer group divides partitions among its members, each member pulls records and commits its position, and Kafka moves partitions to other members when one stops.

offset 1

Mental model

Think of a team that reads six notebooks. Each notebook has one reader at a time, and each reader keeps a bookmark in the notebook. If a reader leaves, someone else takes the notebook and continues from the bookmark. If the bookmark was not moved recently, the new reader reads some pages again.

  • The partition count is the limit of parallel work. Six partitions can feed at most six consumers in one group. A seventh has nothing to do.
  • The committed offset is the bookmark. It is the offset of the next record to read, not the last one read.
  • A rebalance is the moment partitions change owner. It happens when a member joins, leaves, or is judged dead.
  • Lag is the distance from the bookmark to the end of the notebook.

Three delivery guarantees

The order of two actions, "process the record" and "commit the offset", gives the guarantee.

NameOrderAfter a crash
At most onceCommit, then processRecords that were committed but not processed are lost.
At least onceProcess, then commitRecords that were processed but not committed are processed again.
Exactly onceProcess and commit in one atomic stepNeither. This needs transactions (module 06) or a processing step that is safe to repeat.

How it works internally

The poll loop

All consumer work happens inside one call that your code repeats: poll().

  1. Join. On the first poll, the consumer finds the group's coordinator, a broker that manages this group, and joins the group.
  2. Get an assignment. The consumer learns which partitions it owns.
  3. Find the start. For each partition it asks for the committed offset. If there is none, auto.offset.reset decides: latest (the default) or earliest.
  4. Fetch. It sends fetch requests to the leaders of its partitions. The broker answers when it has fetch.min.bytes of data or after fetch.max.wait.ms.
  5. Return records. poll() hands your code up to max.poll.records records.
  6. Process and commit. Your code processes them. The offset is committed by you, or by auto-commit during a later poll.

A consumer is not safe to use from several threads. One thread owns it and calls poll().

Where offsets are stored

Committed offsets are records in an internal topic, __consumer_offsets. It has 50 partitions and uses compaction, so it keeps the newest offset for each group, topic and partition. The coordinator for a group is the broker that leads one of those 50 partitions: the hash of the group id picks which one.

A group's offsets are deleted when the group has had no members for offsets.retention.minutes, 7 days by default. After that, the group starts again from auto.offset.reset.

How Kafka knows a consumer is alive

CheckDetectsLimit
HeartbeatThe process or its network is dead. A background thread sends heartbeats.No heartbeat for the session timeout, 45 seconds.
Poll intervalThe process is alive but your code is stuck or too slow.No poll() call for max.poll.interval.ms, 5 minutes.

When either limit is passed, the consumer leaves the group and its partitions go to other members.

Two rebalance protocols

Kafka 4 has two ways to run a group. The consumer chooses with group.protocol.

Classic (group.protocol=classic, the default)New (group.protocol=consumer)
Who computes the assignmentOne consumer in the group, chosen as leader, using partition.assignment.strategy.The broker, using group.remote.assignor: uniform or range.
How a change happensEvery member must rejoin. With the older strategies, every member first gives up all its partitions.The broker tells each member only what to give up and what to take. Members that are not affected continue.
Where timeouts are setOn the client: session.timeout.ms, heartbeat.interval.ms.On the broker: group.consumer.session.timeout.ms, group.consumer.heartbeat.interval.ms. The two client settings are not supported.
Effect of one slow memberIt can hold the whole rebalance.It delays only its own partitions.

The demo at the top of this page shows the difference. "Stop everything" is the classic protocol with an older strategy such as RangeAssignor. "Move only what must move" is the new protocol, and also the classic protocol with CooperativeStickyAssignor.

Kafka 4.3 prints this line when a console consumer starts: "The consumer rebalance protocol (KIP-848) is production-ready! Set group.protocol=consumer to try it out."

Static membership

Each restart of a consumer normally causes two rebalances: one when it leaves and one when it returns. If you give each consumer a fixed name with group.instance.id, the coordinator keeps its partitions reserved until the session timeout. A restart that is faster than the timeout causes no rebalance. This helps rolling deployments.

Lag

Lag for a partition is the log end offset minus the committed offset. A steady small lag is normal. A lag that grows means the group processes slower than producers write. If the lag grows for longer than the retention time, the oldest unread records are deleted before the consumer reaches them.

Failure scenarios

A slow batch removes the consumer from the group

What breaks
The consumer processes a batch for longer than max.poll.interval.ms. It is removed, another member takes its partitions and processes the same records again. Then the first one fails to commit.
You observe
A commit error that says the group has rebalanced. Repeated records. Rebalances every few minutes.
Fix
Lower max.poll.records so that a batch is always fast. Or raise max.poll.interval.ms. Do not move slow work to another thread without also pausing the partition, or you lose the back-pressure.

Rebalance storm

What breaks
The group spends most of its time rebalancing and little time processing. Lag grows.
Cause
A rolling deployment restarts members one after another, or members keep passing the poll interval, or the session timeout is too low for a network with short pauses.
Fix
Use the new protocol or CooperativeStickyAssignor. Set group.instance.id for deployments. Fix the slow processing.

One bad record blocks a partition

What breaks
A record cannot be parsed or always fails in processing. The consumer throws, restarts, reads the same record, and throws again. This is a poison record.
You observe
Lag grows on one partition only. The committed offset does not move.
Fix
Catch the error, send the record to a separate topic for failed records (a dead-letter topic, module 08), commit, and continue. Decide in advance which errors deserve a retry.

Auto-commit loses records

What breaks
Your code hands records to a worker thread and calls poll() again. Auto-commit commits them. The process crashes before the worker has finished.
Result
Those records are never processed.
Fix
Set enable.auto.commit=false and commit only what is finished.

A new group skips existing data

What breaks
A new application starts and processes nothing, although the topic has data.
Cause
auto.offset.reset is latest by default, so a group without offsets starts at the end. The lab shows it: 6,000 records in the topic, 0 read.
Fix
Set earliest for applications that must process the history.

The offsets of an idle group disappear

What breaks
A job that runs once a month starts from the wrong place.
Cause
The group was empty for more than offsets.retention.minutes, 7 days. Its offsets were deleted.
Fix
Raise the broker setting, or store the position yourself, or keep one member running.

More consumers than partitions

What breaks
You scale the application from 6 to 12 instances and throughput does not change.
Cause
The topic has 6 partitions. Six instances own one each. The other six own nothing.
Fix
Add partitions (remember the ordering cost from module 01), or make each consumer faster, or use share groups (module 10) when order does not matter.

Common mistakes and misconceptions

People thinkWhat is true
Kafka pushes records to consumers.Consumers pull. The consumer controls its own speed.
The committed offset is the last record processed.It is the next record to read: the last processed offset plus one.
More consumers always means more throughput.Only up to the partition count. Extra members of a group are idle.
Auto-commit gives at-least-once.Only if your code finishes every record before the next poll(). With worker threads it can lose records.
Two groups share the records between them.Each group gets every record. Sharing happens only inside one group.
A heartbeat proves the consumer is working.It proves the process is alive. A stuck processing loop still sends heartbeats; the poll interval catches that.
Lag in records tells you how late you are.It tells you how many records. How late depends on the write rate. Measure lag in time too.
A consumer can be shared between threads.It cannot. Use one consumer for each thread, or one polling thread that hands work to others.
offset 7

Staff-level questions

Each record takes 2 seconds to process. How do you design the consumer?

First check the numbers: 500 records in one poll at 2 seconds each is 1,000 seconds, far past the 5-minute poll interval. So lower max.poll.records to a value that finishes well inside the interval, for example 50.

For more throughput, add partitions and consumers. If that is not enough, process records in a worker pool inside each consumer: pause the partition, hand out the records, call poll() to keep the membership alive, commit only the offset below which all records are finished, then resume. Keep records with the same key on the same worker if order matters.

How do you make processing safe to repeat?

At-least-once is the practical default, so repeats will happen. Give every event a unique id. In the database, write the result and the event id in one transaction, with a unique constraint on the id. A repeat then fails the constraint and is skipped.

Other options: make the operation naturally repeatable ("set balance to 50" instead of "add 10"), or store the Kafka offset together with the result and skip anything at or below it.

Should a new service use the classic or the new group protocol?

On Kafka 4 brokers with a 4.x Java client, use the new one: only affected partitions stop, a slow member cannot hold the group, and timeouts are managed in one place on the broker.

Check two things first. Clients in other languages may not support it yet. And a custom client-side assignor does not carry over, because the broker now computes the assignment.

What do you alert on for a consumer group?

Lag in time, not only in records, compared with what the business accepts and with the retention time. The rate of rebalances. The time since the last commit for each partition, which catches a poison record. And the number of members compared with the expected number.

A fixed threshold on record lag gives false alarms on busy topics and misses problems on quiet ones.

A deployment causes 10 minutes of duplicates each time. Why, and what do you change?

Each restart triggers rebalances. With an eager strategy, every member gives up its partitions; anything processed but not yet committed is processed again by the new owner.

Changes, in order of effect: commit when partitions are revoked; use the new protocol or the cooperative strategy so that only moved partitions are affected; set group.instance.id so that a quick restart is not a rebalance at all; and shut down cleanly so that the consumer commits and leaves.

When does reading from a follower make sense?

In a cluster that spans zones or regions, where traffic between zones costs money or time. With client.rack set on the consumer and the matching selector on the brokers, the consumer fetches from a follower in its own zone.

The cost is a small extra delay, because a follower learns the high watermark slightly after the leader.

Self-test

1. A topic has 4 partitions and a group has 6 consumers. How many consumers receive records?

Four. Two are idle.

2. A consumer has processed offset 41. Which offset does it commit?

42: the next record to read.

3. Where are committed offsets stored?

In the compacted internal topic __consumer_offsets, which has 50 partitions.

4. A new group reads a topic that already has data and receives nothing. Why?

auto.offset.reset is latest by default, so the group starts at the end.

5. Which order of process and commit gives at-least-once?

Process first, then commit.

6. What does max.poll.interval.ms protect against?

A consumer that is alive but stuck or too slow: it sends heartbeats but does not call poll().

7. Name two differences between the classic and the new group protocol.

In the new protocol the broker computes the assignment, and only the affected partitions stop during a change. Session and heartbeat timeouts are set on the broker.

8. What does group.instance.id change?

The member becomes static. A restart that is shorter than the session timeout causes no rebalance.

9. Which command shows the lag of a group?

kafka-consumer-groups.sh --describe --group followed by the group name.

10. What must be true before you can reset a group's offsets with the tool?

The group has no active members.
