Multi-cluster and design

What it is and why it exists

One cluster in one place is the simple case. Three needs push beyond it.

  • Surviving the loss of a whole site. Replication inside a cluster (module 04) protects against a broker failure. It does not protect against the loss of the data centre or region that the cluster is in.
  • Moving data between places. Applications in one region need data produced in another. Analytics needs a copy that heavy queries cannot disturb. A migration needs two clusters in step for a while.
  • Work queues. A consumer group ties parallel work to the partition count and delivers in order. Some work, such as sending emails or resizing images, wants the opposite: many workers on any record, with each record acknowledged alone.

Kafka's answers are MirrorMaker 2, which copies topics from one cluster to another; stretch clusters, where one cluster spans several sites; and share groups, a second kind of group that gives Kafka queue behaviour.

The module ends with the most senior skill: saying when Kafka is not the right tool.

The one-sentence version. Copy between clusters when sites must fail independently, stretch one cluster when no record may be lost and the sites are close, use share groups when work is independent, and choose something simpler when you do not need a replayable log.

A copy is not the original

MirrorMaker 2 is a consumer on one cluster and a producer on the other. So the copy is a new topic with its own offsets. Offset 500 in the source is not offset 500 in the copy. This one fact causes most of the difficulty: a consumer that moves to the other cluster cannot reuse its committed offsets, so they must be translated.

How it works internally

MirrorMaker 2

MirrorMaker 2 is a set of Kafka Connect connectors (module 08). The lab's Connect worker listed all three among its plug-ins.

ConnectorJob
Source connectorCopies records, and also creates the topics on the target with the same partition count and settings.
Checkpoint connectorTranslates consumer group offsets from the source to the target.
Heartbeat connectorWrites a regular marker so that you can check that the link is alive and measure its delay.
  1. You name each cluster with an alias, such as main and dr, and declare a flow: main->dr.
  2. By default the copy of a topic gets the source alias as a prefix: orders becomes main.orders. The prefix shows where data came from and stops a loop when two clusters copy to each other.
  3. A record keeps its key, value, headers, timestamp and partition number. It gets a new offset.
  4. The source connector writes pairs of matching offsets, source and target, to an offset-syncs topic from time to time.
  5. The checkpoint connector reads each group's committed offsets on the source, uses those pairs to compute a safe offset on the target, and writes it to a checkpoints topic. With sync.group.offsets.enabled it also commits it for the group on the target.

Translation is deliberately cautious. In the lab, the group g-new was at offset 1261 on a source partition and was given 1112 on the copy. After a failover it would read 149 records again. It would not skip any. So a consumer that fails over must tolerate repeats.

The copy is asynchronous. A producer gets its answer from the source cluster before the record reaches the target. In the lab a new record reached the second cluster about 2 seconds later. Whatever is in flight when the source is lost, is lost.

Stretch clusters

A stretch cluster is one normal cluster whose brokers and controllers are placed in three sites. With broker.rack set, each partition has a replica in each site. With acks=all and a minimum of 2, a record is in two sites before it is acknowledged. Losing a site loses no committed record, and failover is the ordinary leader election from module 04. Clients do nothing.

The price is in the network. Every write waits for a round trip between sites, and the controllers need low delay to each other. This works between zones of one cloud region, a millisecond or two apart. It does not work between continents.

Share groups

  1. A consumer joins a share group and is assigned partitions. Several members can have the same partition; in the lab both workers showed jobs:0.
  2. When a member fetches, the broker hands it some available records and marks them as acquired by that member, with a time limit: the lock, 30 seconds by default.
  3. The member processes each record and acknowledges it: accept (done), release (give it back for another try), or reject (give up on it).
  4. If the lock runs out with no acknowledgement, the record becomes available again and another member can take it.
  5. Each record has a delivery count. After 5 deliveries without success, the broker stops offering it.
  6. The broker keeps this per-record state in an internal topic, __share_group_state.

Because records are handed out one batch at a time to whoever asks, order is not kept, and there is no per-partition offset for the application to commit. The group's position is a start offset, below which every record is finished.

The lab cluster reports the feature share.version at level 1, so share groups are on. They are new in the 4.x line. Check the release notes for the status in the version you run, and whether your client library supports them. unverified: I did not confirm the release in which they became production-ready

Failure scenarios

The copy contains aborted transactions

What breaks
Consumers on the second cluster receive records that were rolled back on the first.
Evidence
In the lab, payments had 78 offsets on the source and main.payments had 73 on the copy. The source held 73 data records and 5 transaction markers. Of the 73 data records, 38 belonged to the transaction that was aborted in module 06. They were copied as ordinary records, and the markers were not.
Cause
The copying consumer reads with the default isolation level, read_uncommitted.
Fix
Set the source consumer's isolation level to read_committed in the MirrorMaker 2 configuration for any flow that carries transactional topics. unverified: I did not rerun the lab with this setting

Records are lost in a failover

What breaks
The main site is lost. Records that were acknowledged there but not yet copied do not exist on the standby.
Cause
Copying between clusters is asynchronous.
Fix
Measure the copy delay all the time and agree that this delay is your possible data loss. If no loss is acceptable, you need a stretch cluster, or the producing application must be able to send again from its own source of truth.

Consumers repeat or skip after a failover

What breaks
A consumer starts on the standby at the wrong place.
Cause
Offsets differ between clusters. Translated offsets are cautious, so some records repeat: 149 for one partition in the lab. If offset translation was not set up, the consumer starts from auto.offset.reset, which can skip everything or repeat everything.
Fix
Turn on offset translation for the groups that must fail over. Make consumers safe to repeat. Remember the topic name changes to the prefixed form unless you use the identity policy.

Failback is harder than failover

What breaks
After the main site returns, the standby holds records the main site never saw, and the main site may hold records that were never copied.
Fix
Plan the return path before you need it. Often the simplest safe plan is to treat the standby as the new main, rebuild the old main as its standby from scratch, and switch back in a quiet period. Practise it.

Two active clusters update the same thing

What breaks
The same customer is changed in both regions at once. Each region applies the other's change late. The regions end with different values.
Cause
There is no order between two clusters.
Fix
Give each entity a home region that owns its writes, or design updates that can be merged in any order. This is an application design problem that Kafka cannot solve for you.

A stretch cluster with a slow link

What breaks
Write latency rises for every producer, followers fall out of sync, and controller elections start.
Cause
The delay between sites is too high or varies.
Fix
Use a stretch cluster only across sites with a short, steady delay. Measure the 99th percentile, not the average.

A share group job is done twice, or gives up

What breaks
A job takes longer than the lock. The broker offers it to a second worker while the first is still working. Or a job fails 5 times and is dropped.
Fix
Keep jobs shorter than the lock time, or raise the lock within its allowed range. Make jobs safe to repeat. Decide what happens to a job that reaches the delivery limit: at the least, log it where someone will see it.

Kafka was the wrong choice

You observe
A team spends more time on the cluster than on the product. Topics carry a few records each hour. Applications wait on a reply topic to imitate a function call.
Fix
Use the checklist in this module at design time. For small volumes, a table in the database you already run, or a managed queue, is cheaper to own.

Common mistakes and misconceptions

People thinkWhat is true
Three copies in one cluster is disaster recovery.It protects against a broker failure. A site failure takes all three unless the cluster spans sites.
A mirrored topic has the same offsets.It has its own. Consumer positions must be translated.
Copying between clusters loses nothing.It is asynchronous. Whatever was in flight is lost with the source.
MirrorMaker 2 respects transactions by default.In the lab it copied aborted records as ordinary records.
Active-active gives the best of everything.It moves the hard problem, conflicting updates, into your application.
A disaster plan that was never practised is a plan.It is a hope. Run the failover, and the return, on a schedule.
Share groups replace consumer groups.They are for independent jobs. Events that need order still need consumer groups.
Every asynchronous problem needs Kafka.Kafka is a replayable log for many readers at high volume. Without those needs, simpler tools cost less to own.

Staff-level questions

The business says "we cannot lose any payment event, even if a region is lost". What do you propose?

First make the requirement precise: zero loss of acknowledged records, and how long may the service be down? Zero loss rules out asynchronous copying alone.

Option one: a stretch cluster over three zones of one region, with a minimum of 2 and acks=all. It survives a zone, not a region. Option two, for a real region loss: keep the source of truth in a database that is itself replicated synchronously, publish through an outbox, and treat Kafka in the second region as rebuildable from that source. Then Kafka's asynchronous copy only decides how fast you recover, not what you lose.

State the cost of each plainly. Synchronous replication across regions makes every payment slower, all year, to protect against a rare event.

Design the failover runbook for an active and standby pair.

Before: copy delay and heartbeat are monitored; offset translation is on for the groups that matter; consumers are safe to repeat; clients can be pointed at the standby by configuration, and know the prefixed topic names or use the identity policy.

During: decide, by a named person and a stated rule, that the main site is lost. Stop the copy so that a half-alive source cannot write. Point producers at the standby. Start consumers there from the translated offsets. Record the time and the last copied offsets for each topic: that is the possible loss.

After: do not switch back in a hurry. Rebuild the old site as the new standby, let it catch up, and return in a planned window. Then review what was lost and what repeated.

When would you choose share groups over a traditional message queue product?

When you already run Kafka and the jobs are produced from data that is in Kafka: one platform, one security model, and the same records can also be read as a log by other applications.

A dedicated queue is still the better fit when you need features that are native there: priorities, a delay for each message, routing by content, a built-in dead-letter queue, or strict order within a group of messages together with many competing workers. Check also the maturity of share groups in your client language.

How do you migrate every application from an old cluster to a new one with no downtime?

Copy all topics from old to new with MirrorMaker 2, using the identity policy so that names stay the same, and with offset translation. Wait until the copy delay is steady and low.

Move consumers first, group by group: stop a group on the old cluster, start it on the new one from its translated offsets. It repeats a few records. Then move producers. For a topic with keys, move all its producers together, or records for one key will be written on both sides out of order. When the old cluster's topics receive nothing and are fully copied, stop the copy and retire the old cluster.

A team wants to use Kafka for request and reply between two services. What do you say?

It works, and it is usually the wrong shape. The caller must create a reply topic or partition, match replies to requests with an id, handle timeouts and late replies, and it pays the latency of two produce and fetch round trips. Failures are harder to see than a failed call.

If the caller needs an answer now, use a direct call. Use Kafka where the sender does not need to wait: publish the fact, and let the other service react. If they want both, do the call directly and publish an event about the result.

What would you put on one page as "how we use Kafka here"?

Topic standards: naming, three copies, a minimum of 2, how partition counts are chosen, who owns each topic. Client standards: acks=all, idempotence, a client id, handling of send errors, consumers that are safe to repeat, explicit auto.offset.reset. Data standards: a schema for every topic with a stated compatibility mode. Failure standards: dead-letter topics with owners, lag alerts in time, a practised recovery plan. And a short list of what Kafka is not to be used for.

These are the decisions from all ten modules. Writing them down once is what stops each team from learning them through an incident.

Self-test

1. What do recovery point objective and recovery time objective measure?

How much recent data may be lost, and how long the service may be down.

2. What is MirrorMaker 2 built on?

Kafka Connect: a source connector, a checkpoint connector and a heartbeat connector.

3. The topic orders is copied from a cluster with the alias main. What is it called on the target by default?

main.orders.

4. Why can a consumer not reuse its committed offsets on the other cluster?

The copy is a new topic with its own offsets. They must be translated.

5. Is copying between clusters synchronous?

No. The producer is answered before the record reaches the other cluster.

6. What makes a stretch cluster lose no committed record when a site fails?

It is one cluster with replicas in each site, acks=all and a minimum of 2, so each record is in two sites before it is acknowledged.

7. What can a share group do that a consumer group cannot?

Let several members read the same partition, with each record acknowledged alone.

8. What happens to a share group record whose lock runs out?

It becomes available again and another member can take it, until the delivery limit, 5 by default.

9. What does a share group give up?

Order.

10. Name two signs that a use case does not need Kafka.

Any two of: the caller waits for a reply; low volume with a database already at hand; messages need priorities or delays; nobody will own the cluster.
